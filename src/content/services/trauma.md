When our bodies experience trauma, they instinctively shut down functions that are not essential for survival at that moment. While this response may protect us during a crisis, keeping those functions suppressed and continuing to live in **crisis mode** does not support long-term survival, healing, and overall vitality. When this **shutdown** becomes continuous, our bodies become stuck in repeating, ineffective, false patterns where we continue to experience trauma-induced emotions, cognitions, behaviors, and even physiological responses or patterns of regulation. Even though we're no longer experiencing the original trauma, our bodies continue to respond as though we are. Functioning like you’re in trauma all the time is an ineffective way to live life.

**Feelings are not facts.** Trauma likes to let our limbic system pretend that it is in charge. But it's not the boss of you. We can identify and work with how your body has experienced trauma. We can rewire previously established pathways so that they function more effectively. This is **neuroplasticity**, and it happens daily. Let's work together to give your brain the chance to change.

My preferred method for treating trauma is **[Lifespan Integration](https://www.lifespanintegration.us/origins-and-development-of-lifespan-integration/)**. I believe in **neuroplasticity** and the body's ability to create new neural pathways that allow us to live in the present rather than remain in a trauma response. In the present, you get to choose. Trauma does not have to keep playing out in your brain, your life, or your relationships. It did happen, but it doesn't have to keep happening.
