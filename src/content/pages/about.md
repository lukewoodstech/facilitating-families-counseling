I grew up on a farm in northern Wyoming. Farms in Wyoming are brown. Getting water to fields so that they turn green takes effort—a lot of effort. I grew up understanding that change requires hard work.

I don’t mind hard work or change. I am the result of generations of changers—individuals who worked tirelessly to not just improve the land but also improve life. Life improvements impacted their present and my future. These are my roots.

I attended Brigham Young University, where I studied Family Sciences as an undergraduate and then received my graduate degree in Marriage and Family Therapy. I’ve had the privilege of working with individuals and families while my husband and I raised our five children in Wyoming, Colorado, Minnesota, and Utah. Being a mom requires skills! I like to think of motherhood as my residency in Family Therapy, except this residency lasts a lifetime! I wouldn’t have it any other way. It’s probably where I learned the most about working with families. The lessons I learned in the trenches—or the lessons I’m still learning—have been a critical resource in my professional work. They help me get it. While we lived in Minnesota, I worked at the Melrose Center, a hospital specializing in the treatment of eating disorders.

I believe all mortals have vulnerabilities. Some are just visible. Bonnie is probably my most visible vulnerability. Bonnie is my power chair. She’s blue at the base. My experience with Bonnie has taught me things I would have never thought about learning. I always tell others that although it will be miraculous when I walk, the true miracle is that I haven’t gone crazy sitting!

Pre-Bonnie, my hobbies included skiing, wrapping presents, entertaining children with simple science experiments, cleaning, and organizing. My body no longer allows me to do those fun things, so I’ve found new ways to channel my curiosity and energy. Today, I spend much of my time learning about how our minds and bodies work. They are simply amazing! I am continually humbled and fascinated by their incredible capacity! I am also quite aware that things change, and I’m eager for the changes that will allow me to get back to my old hobbies. Although, when I ask my husband what we’re going to do when I get out of this wheelchair, he says, “I hope not more cleaning!”

Most of all, I love people. I love learning about them, understanding what makes them who they are, and watching them make meaningful, lasting changes in their lives.
